## Calculating Entry Points for Stocks

There are several methods traders and investors use to determine a good entry point:

---

### 1. **Technical Analysis Methods**

**Support Levels**
The price tends to "bounce" from historical support zones. Look for areas where the stock has reversed upward multiple times — these are natural buy zones.

**Moving Averages**
- Buy when price pulls back to the 50-day or 200-day moving average
- A crossover (e.g., 50-day crossing above 200-day — "golden cross") signals a potential entry

**RSI (Relative Strength Index)**
- RSI below 30 = oversold → potential buy signal
- Best used in combination with support levels

**Breakout Entry**
- Enter when the stock breaks above a key resistance level with strong volume
- Common in momentum trading

---

### 2. **Fundamental Analysis Methods**

**Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio**
Compare the stock's P/E to its historical average or sector peers. Buying when P/E is below its historical mean suggests undervaluation.

**Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)**
Calculate the intrinsic value of the stock. Enter when market price is significantly below intrinsic value (the "margin of safety").

**52-Week Low Strategy**
Stocks near their 52-week low may be undervalued — but always verify *why* they're low first.

---

### 3. **Risk-Based Entry (Most Practical)**

This is a disciplined approach:

1. **Identify your stop-loss** — the price at which you'd exit if wrong (e.g., below a support level)
2. **Calculate risk per share** → `Entry Price − Stop Loss Price`
3. **Set position size** based on how much capital you're willing to risk (e.g., 1-2% of portfolio)
4. **Enter at a price where the risk/reward ratio is at least 1:2 or 1:3**

> Example: Entry at ₹100, Stop Loss at ₹95, Target at ₹115 → Risk = ₹5, Reward = ₹15 → Ratio = 1:3 ✓

---

### 4. **Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)**

If you're unsure about the exact entry point (common for long-term investors):
- Buy a fixed amount at regular intervals (weekly/monthly)
- Averages out your cost over time, reducing timing risk

---

### Quick Summary Table

| Goal | Best Method |
|---|---|
| Long-term investing | DCF + P/E + DCA |
| Swing trading | Support levels + Moving averages |
| Momentum trading | Breakout + Volume confirmation |
| Risk management | Stop-loss based entry |

The most reliable entries usually combine **at least one technical signal** with **a fundamental reason** to own the stock.
Never enter based on a single indicator alone.
